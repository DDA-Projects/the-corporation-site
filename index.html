The Corporation

Balance out your career and life.

Six months' probation in an open-plan office, one day at a time. Two answers a day. Neither is the right one.

Play in your browser See what happens

Coming to iPhone and Android.

You are the Junior now.
The chair is still warm.

Each morning somebody walks over to your desk — your manager, Marta from the team that keeps shipping your work, HR with a face you learn to read — and puts a situation in front of you. You get two ways to answer it.

Say yes to everything and your body goes first. Coast and the reviews notice, then HR notices, then a file exists with your name on the tab. The office is keeping score in ways it will never show you.

What you actually do

  • Answer one situation a day, from over a hundred, drawn to fit where your career is.
  • Spend the evening on a date, on friends, on something of your own, or on your phone.
  • Watch five meters you can read and several you cannot.
  • Sit your Friday one-on-one and take the grade.
  • Take the job at the other company, or tear the letter up.

What the office does

  • HR opens a file, writes it up, and closes it again — and tells you, every time.
  • Your manager decides whether you exist this quarter.
  • A reorg arrives. Your salary drifts. Your project goes to somebody else.
  • A firing is never a surprise: the warning comes first.
  • Afterwards the game tells you exactly which part of the score did it.
